How this was made

  • “Distinctive” means a phrase this speaker said much more often than everyone else at the hearing, including the rest of their own group, after allowing for how much each side talked. A phrase someone says often but everyone else also says often is not distinctive.
  • Each phrase gets a score from the “weighted log-odds” test in Monroe, Colaresi and Quinn (2008), “Fightin' Words: Lexical Feature Selection and Evaluation for Identifying the Content of Political Conflict,” Political Analysis 16(4). We keep phrases said at least 2 times with a score above 1.96, a common cutoff for a difference that is unlikely to be chance. When a short phrase almost always appears inside a longer one, only the longer one is shown.
  • Counts are how many times each 1- to 3-word phrase occurs in the clean verbatim transcript. Common words, speakers' names, “New York”, “city” and hearing boilerplate (“thank you, chair”) are left out.
  • Turns are assigned to a speaker by the speaker labels, which were assigned by AI agents working from self-introductions, the chair calling on people, and voice clusters, at BetaNYC's direction. A mislabeled turn counts toward the wrong speaker.
  • This is a machine transcript, not an official record. Check the video before quoting anyone; search the clean transcript to find where a phrase was said.
  • Data: logan-graham.csv (every phrase that passed). Code: scripts/build_wordclouds.py.
