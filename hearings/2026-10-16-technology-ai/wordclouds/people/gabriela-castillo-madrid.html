How this was made

  • “Distinctive” means a phrase this speaker said much more often than everyone else at the hearing, including the rest of their own group, after allowing for how much each side talked. A phrase someone says often but everyone else also says often is not distinctive.
  • Each phrase gets a score from the “weighted log-odds” test in Monroe, Colaresi and Quinn (2008), “Fightin' Words: Lexical Feature Selection and Evaluation for Identifying the Content of Political Conflict,” Political Analysis 16(4). We keep phrases said at least 2 times with a score above 1.96, a common cutoff; with thousands of phrases tested, a few may pass by chance, so read the list as a guide, not proof. When a short phrase almost always appears inside a longer one, only the longer one is shown.
  • Counts are how many times each 1- to 3-word phrase occurs in the clean verbatim transcript. Hyphenated words are counted as separate words (“self-improvement” is “self improvement”), and a few terms the transcript spells more than one way are counted as one (“super intelligent” as “superintelligent”, “kill switches” as “kill switch”, “open-weight” and “affected parties” as one term).
  • Common words, speakers' names, “New York”, “city”, “AI” and hearing boilerplate are left out, using the word lists made for the 2026-10-05 hearing, which include filler an AI assistant proposed cutting from that hearing's lists. This hearing's lists were not reviewed by hand. Word lists: scripts/meta/wordclouds.json; this hearing's groups: scripts/meta/2026-10-16-technology-ai-wordclouds.json.
  • Turns are assigned to a speaker by the speaker labels, which were assigned by AI agents working from self-introductions, the chair calling on people, and voice clusters, at BetaNYC's direction. A mislabeled turn counts toward the wrong speaker.
  • 56 turns at this hearing hold two voices; their words count toward the person named on the turn, so a cloud can include some words said by another speaker. On this page, 1,211 of the 4,150 words are in such turns.
  • This is a machine transcript, not an official record. Check the video before quoting anyone; search the clean transcript to find where a phrase was said.
  • Data: gabriela-castillo-madrid.csv (every phrase that passed). Code: scripts/build_wordclouds.py.
