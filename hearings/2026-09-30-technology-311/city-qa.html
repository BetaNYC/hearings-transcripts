Thank you. Good afternoon, Chair De La Rosa and members of the City Council Committee on Technology. My name is Joe Morrisroe, and I am the Deputy Commissioner of New York City 311. As you know, NYC 311 delivers fast and easy access to government services and information with the highest possible level of customer service. 311 is available twenty-four hours a day, seven days a week, three hundred sixty-five days a year through multiple channels, including the call center, online portal, text, mobile application, and social media. Originally launched as a call center, New York City 311 has evolved into the most comprehensive municipal government customer service platform in the nation. 311 received 37,000,000 customer contacts in 2025 and on an annual basis receives more calls than all US city 311 call centers combined. I'm also pleased to share with the Council a recent report by an independent third party on the public satisfaction with New York City 311. Call center representatives received a customer satisfaction rating of 93%, and 90% of the respondents stated they are likely to recommend 311, which outperforms best-in-class private sector companies. The level of public satisfaction is a testament to the dedication and the commitment of the New York City 311 team that serves their fellow New Yorkers every day. It is important to note that 311 serves as the platform to provide information and services to the public. With few exceptions, public interactions with 311 result in one of the following outcomes. The customer has an information request, such as a garbage pickup day, and the information is provided. A customer is referred to an external entity, which can include a utility or a state or federal agency. And a customer's inquiry is submitted as a service request to the designated agency and a confirmation tracking number is provided. We rely on our collaborative relationships with each city agency to build out service request forms and information pages, which we also call knowledge articles, those that properly reflect the mission and services for each agency or office. 311 does not create service requests unilaterally. There must be an agency that will accept the request, investigate, and, if applicable, undertake appropriate action or resolution. Once a service request is submitted through New York City 311, it becomes the responding agency's responsibility to address and respond. When an agency decides that amending a current service request or adding a new one is necessary, it contacts 311 to create, test, and deploy a service request form. Agency feedback partially informs the update we implement to the entire 311 platform. User feedback is also extremely useful to us, which is why we deploy customer surveys that inform the Resolution Satisfaction dashboard, which goes by the acronym RSAT, which is available on the city's website. We also value the input we receive from Council Members as frequent 311 users, and I'm happy to continue the conversation. I will now turn to the legislation associated with this hearing. Intro 173 would require the use of global positioning system, GPS, coordinates for 311 service requests. We agree that our user interface would be improved with the capability to capture a user's coordinates with the familiar pin drop that many location-based applications use. Currently, 311 service requests collect GPS coordinates, latitude and longitude, based on the customer enabling location services in the mobile app or on the website and the customer confirming the address that is identified. If location services functionality is disabled, 311 collects latitude and longitude based on the address entered by the customer. We have been exploring how our geolocator service could collect lat and long based on a pin drop, then identify the address in the format required by the agency based on the service request. For example, an address, an intersection, or a block face. I am looking forward to working with the Council to find the best path forward for this enhancement. Introduction 669 proposes to refer 311 nonemergency traffic condition complaints to the Transportation Bureau of the Police Department. As I noted above, we work with each agency to develop service requests to work best for the customer and agency policies and workflows that support their responsibilities. For the types of complaints listed in this legislation, the protocol is similar. If a customer describes an emergency condition in progress, the call is transferred to 911. A call describing a nonemergency situation will generate a service request that is routed to the appropriate NYPD precinct.
